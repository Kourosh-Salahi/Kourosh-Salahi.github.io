Colorizing the Prokudin-Gorskii photo collection

Aligning three monochrome glass-plate channels (B, G, R) into color images using single-scale search and coarse-to-fine image pyramids, scored with normalized cross-correlation.

Overview

Given three images taken in black and white with R, G, and B filters placed over the camera lens, we can use the pixel intensities of these three images to reconstruct a color image. This was accomplished with an alignment algorithm.

Scoring metric. I implemented normalized cross-correlation (NCC) as the scoring metric. It worked well throughout.

Method

Below is the end-to-end pipeline I used.

1. Preprocessing

  • Split B/G/R: each input plate is divided into three equal vertical slices in B, G, R order.
  • Type and scale: convert to float so metrics are comparable across images.
  • Interior crop for scoring: ignore roughly 10% of the border on each side when computing alignment scores, to avoid frame artifacts and scan edges.

2. Single-scale exhaustive search

  • For each channel C in {G, R}, search integer displacements with np.roll over (dx, dy) in [-w, w], where w is the displacement window being searched.
  • NCC: ⟨B̂, Ĉ⟩ = (B · C) / (‖B‖ ‖C‖), computed by flattening channels into vectors. Higher is better.
  • Pick the displacement with the best score and record (dx, dy) for R to B and G to B.

3. Coarse-to-fine image pyramid

  • Build pyramid: repeatedly downsample by 2 (with anti-aliasing) down to 1/16 scale.
  • Coarsest match: run the single-scale matcher to get an initial (dx, dy).
  • Upscale and refine: multiply displacements by 2 when moving up a level and refine in a small window (about ±4) around the upscaled estimate.
  • Rationale: the pyramid reduces the number of candidate shifts dramatically while avoiding local minima from large motions.

4. Composition

  • Shift G and R by the chosen displacements using np.roll and stack channels into [R, G, B].
  • For visualization, optionally crop the outer margins to remove wraparound introduced by shifting.

Scoring notes and design choices

  • Metric choice: NCC is generally more robust when channels have different brightness or contrast; L2 serves as a simple baseline.
  • Interior crop: ignoring borders prevents frame edges from dominating the score and helps with plates that have thick margins.
  • Vectorization: all per-candidate scores are computed with NumPy operations; loops only iterate over candidate shifts.

Failure cases and analysis

Some images had slight alignment effects that can be attributed to people, animals, or scenes such as water moving between shots. The only image that seemed to fail completely in aligning was the image of the Emir. I believe this is due to the following:

  • Image artifacts: some channels had scratches while others did not, potentially causing the optimization to match patterns that did not correspond to real things in the image.
  • Cross-channel brightness: the Emir, in his eternal swagger, has a very bright garment which appears very differently across the color channels (very bright in blue and very dark in red).

Reproducibility and notes

While the code for this is not publicly available, I encourage anyone who stumbles upon this and is not part of CS 180 to try reimplementing it. It is a very fun exercise.
